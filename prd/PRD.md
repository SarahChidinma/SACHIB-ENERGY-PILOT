## 1. Executive Summary

**SACHIB Energy** is an AI-powered energy intelligence and remote-monitoring platform designed to help churches, church-owned institutions, businesses, families, and members manage solar energy systems more reliably and affordably. The platform monitors, predicts, and optimizes energy usage and availability, reducing dependence on diesel generators and unstable grid power.

Independent market research conducted ahead of this document confirms strong demand: the surveyed audience reports frequent and disruptive power outages, near-universal enthusiasm for the proposed concept, and a clear majority expressing purchase intent at an affordable price point. The primary adoption barriers identified are initial cost and product quality assurance — both of which this document addresses through phased delivery, partnership-based hardware sourcing, and a credibility-first go-to-market approach.

This document consolidates the product foundation, validated market need, target users, proposed solution, user journey, expected impact, and phased roadmap into a single reference for Phase 1 (Q1) planning and stakeholder discussion.

## 3. Background & Rationale

### 3.1 Why This Product Exists
The idea was born from observing a practical problem within the church and the wider community: people are often expected to carry out important work without reliable access to electricity. Churches operate large facilities requiring significant energy — auditoriums, offices, schools, hospitals and healthcare facilities, children's churches, businesses, accommodation, media and production operations, security systems, air-conditioning, and lighting and sound systems.

When electricity is unavailable, organizations turn to diesel or petrol generators, creating significant operating costs that reduce funds available for other activities and investments. At the individual level, unreliable electricity also prevents people from fully utilizing their skills and businesses — for example, someone with the knowledge and market to produce ice cream may still struggle to operate reliably because refrigeration requires dependable power.

The problem therefore goes beyond lighting: energy is an enabler of work, business, ministry, healthcare, education, productivity, and economic opportunity.

### 3.2 Problem Statement
Unreliable electricity supply creates financial, operational, and social challenges for churches, businesses, families, and institutions. Continued dependence on diesel and petrol generators creates substantial recurring fuel and maintenance expenses, particularly where electricity is required for long operating hours across multiple facilities.

Solar energy systems, while an important alternative, can fluctuate due to weather conditions, changing sunlight levels, battery limitations, and variations in demand. Many users cannot know in advance whether their solar system will generate enough energy for expected demand, leading to two failure modes:
1. **Oversized systems** — compensating for uncertainty by increasing capital cost unnecessarily.
2. **Undersized systems** — resulting in inadequate electricity and continued reliance on generators or the grid.

There is also limited ability for users to remotely understand what is happening with their solar system when away from the property. This creates a need for an intelligent energy-management solution that continuously monitors the system, forecasts generation and consumption, anticipates shortages, and recommends optimization actions.

#### The Wider Human and Economic Problem
When electricity is unavailable:
- Businesses operate inconsistently
- Perishable products are lost
- Equipment becomes unusable
- Employees and entrepreneurs lose productive hours
- Churches spend more on backup power
- Schools and offices face disruption
- Healthcare facilities are compromised
- Individuals cannot convert their skills into sustainable businesses

The Church Energy initiative treats reliable energy as infrastructure for ministry, work, business, human development, and economic opportunity.

### 4.2 Electricity Reliability & Outage Frequency

Respondents were asked to rate the reliability of their current electricity supply on a 1 (very unreliable) to 5 (reliable) scale.

#### Electricity Reliability Rating
| Reliability Rating | Respondents | Share |

> **Key Finding:** Four of ten respondents (40%) rated their electricity supply as “very unreliable” — the single most common response — and 70% rated it at the midpoint (3) or below. This confirms unreliable power is a majority experience, not an isolated complaint.

> **Key Finding:** Sixty percent (60%) of respondents experience outages at least 3–4 times per week, and 20% face several outages a day. Combined with reliability ratings, this establishes the electricity problem as frequent and material for this audience — not a minor inconvenience.

## 5. Target Users

The product serves exactly two user types in Q1. All other titles map into one of these two.

### 5.1 User Type 1 — Admin
Manages sites, systems, and people. This includes:
- **Church administrator / Facility / Energy manager:** owns daily operations, modes, and load decisions
- **Designer (Admin subtype):** configures system design — sizing, circuit mapping, presets, and commissioning defaults. Same access as Admin, scoped to design/configuration. Cannot manage billing/org ownership unless also granted Owner rights.

Admin can: onboard sites, connect compatible hardware, configure circuits and service-mode presets, manage Member access, view all data, act on advisories, export audits, and use remote control where hardware supports it.

### 5.2 User Type 2 — Member / Energy User
Uses energy at a home, business, or institution facility. This includes business owners, households / individual members, and staff in schools, clinics, and offices.

Member can: view own site(s), receive alerts and advisories, monitor remotely, track diesel/₦ savings, and act to reduce consumption. Cannot configure systems or manage other users.

### 5.3 User Problem
Users often experience unreliable electricity and high costs from diesel/petrol generators and grid power. Even when they have solar systems, they may not know:
- How much energy they are generating, consuming, or storing
- Whether their battery will last through the night or critical events
- Whether they will have enough power for upcoming activities
- What is happening with their system when away from the property

## 7. Proposed Solution

The first product is an **AI-powered solar energy management and remote-monitoring application**. It connects to compatible solar systems and collects data from equipment such as inverters, batteries, smart meters, and IoT devices, transforming this data into useful intelligence for the user.

### 7.1 Core Capabilities

| Capability | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Monitor** | Real-time solar generation, battery levels, energy consumption, system status, and available power sources. |
| **Predict** | Expected solar generation, expected consumption, battery availability, and potential energy shortages. |
| **Analyze** | Historical energy usage and generation patterns to identify trends and unusual behavior. |
| **Advise** | Practical recommendations to help users conserve and optimize energy. |
| **Optimize** | Help users make better decisions about when and how available energy should be used. |
| **Alert** | Warn users about low battery levels, unusual consumption, poor expected generation, and equipment issues. |
| **Remotely Monitor** | Allow users to view their solar system from anywhere — home, work, or travelling. |
| **Eventually Control** | Where compatible hardware supports it, allow secure remote management of selected energy functions. |

### 7.2 The AI Advantage
- **Conventional Solar App:** *“Your battery is 35%.”*
- **SACHIB Energy App:** *“Your battery is 35%. Based on your current consumption, expected weather, and predicted solar generation, your battery may reach a critical level before tomorrow morning. Reduce non-essential loads tonight to preserve energy for essential operations.”*

> **The Core Differentiator:** The product moves from **monitoring what happened**, to **understanding what is happening**, to **predicting what will happen**, to **helping the user decide what to do next**.

## 8. Primary User Journey

The user connects their compatible solar energy system to the SACHIB Energy App and views their energy situation in real time. The app monitors solar generation, battery levels, consumption, and available power sources; analyzes usage patterns; predicts future energy availability and potential shortages; and provides AI-powered recommendations on how to optimize energy use.

The user can then make informed decisions, reduce unnecessary consumption, minimize dependence on generators, and remotely monitor their energy system from anywhere.

| Step | Phase | User Action / System Response |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Connect** | User connects a compatible solar energy system to the SACHIB Energy App. |
| **2** | **View** | User views real-time energy situation: generation, battery level, consumption, and available power sources. |
| **3** | **Analyze** | App analyzes usage patterns against historical data to surface trends. |
| **4** | **Predict** | App forecasts future energy availability and flags potential shortages. |
| **5** | **Advise** | App delivers AI-powered recommendations to optimize energy use. |
| **6** | **Act** | User makes informed decisions, reduces unnecessary consumption, and minimizes generator dependence. |
| **7** | **Monitor Remotely** | User checks system status from anywhere, at any time. |

## 10. Ecosystem Strategy & Roadmap

The project will develop progressively across five phases. Phase 1 is the focus of this Q1 planning cycle.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    P1["Phase 1: Partnership & Pilot (Q1)"] --> P2["Phase 2: AI Platform"]
    P2 --> P3["Phase 3: Deployment"]
    P3 --> P4["Phase 4: Energy Network"]
    P4 --> P5["Phase 5: Local Manufacturing"]
```

### Phase 1 — Partnership & Pilot (Q1 Focus)
Partner with established solar, inverter, battery, IoT, and technology companies to deploy the initial solution. The objective is to validate the product without requiring immediate in-house hardware manufacturing.

### Phase 2 — AI Platform
Develop and validate the Church Energy AI application, focused on monitoring, forecasting, prediction, optimization, alerts, and remote access.

### Phase 3 — Deployment
Deploy the solution across selected churches, schools, offices, hospitals, businesses, and member households, collecting operational data to continuously improve the platform.

### Phase 4 — Energy Network
Develop broader distribution and service capabilities, potentially including energy products, installation services, maintenance, financing models, energy-as-a-service models, and member access programs.

### Phase 5 — Local Manufacturing
As demand, capital, technical capability, and market validation grow, establish or partner in manufacturing capabilities for solar panels, batteries, inverters, energy-management equipment, and IoT devices — progressively developing local technical capacity and creating additional employment and innovation opportunities.

### 13.2 Local Tooling & Architecture Stack
*Note: In accordance with the prototype assessment constraints, both the application and database run strictly **locally** using mock/test data.*

- **Framework:** Vanilla HTML5, Modern CSS3 (Custom Design System tokens, Glassmorphism, CSS Grid/Flexbox), and Vanilla JavaScript (ES6+ Modules).
- **Database:** Browser-native **IndexedDB** paired with **LocalStorage** (running 100% locally on the client without external server dependencies).
- **Accounts & Authentication:** Local Session Mock Auth (simulating Church Admin vs. Member role profiles with local storage tokens; zero sensitive credentials committed).
- **Files & File Storage:** Local Browser Storage (`IndexedDB` Blob store & Local Filesystem for exportable energy audit CSV reports).
- **Hosting / Environment:** Completely local execution (zero public deployment or external cloud requirement for initial prototype).

---

### 13.3 Documented Agent Steering: Tool Choice & Tradeoff Analysis
*Required for AI Grader cross-referencing:*

- **Tool Choice Examined:** Local Database & Backend Architecture (**Node.js/Express + SQLite** vs. **Client-Side IndexedDB / Web Storage**).
- **Initial Agent Proposal:** The AI initially considered proposing a Node.js/Express local backend with an embedded SQLite file database.
- **Steered Decision by User/Student:** The student steered the AI to choose **Client-Side IndexedDB with LocalStorage** for this prototype phase.
- **Rationale & Tradeoff:** 
  1. *Zero Friction Local Execution:* A pure client-side IndexedDB architecture allows the prototype (`index.html`) to open instantly in any local browser without requiring Node.js installations, npm package downloads, or background server processes to run during grading.
  2. *Security & Privacy:* Eliminates risks of accidentally committing sensitive SQLite binaries, credentials, or local environment configurations to the public GitHub repository.
  3. *Adequacy for Prototype Scope:* Meets the single-page prototype requirement completely while providing structured key-value and object storage for simulated inverter telemetry and church load schedules.

### 13.5 Documented Agent Steering: Prototype Scope & Features (`index.html`)
*Required for AI Grader cross-referencing:*

- **Initial Agent Proposal:** The AI initially proposed implementing complex multi-view routing, authentication screens, and backend API endpoints.
- **Steered Decision by User/Student:** The student steered the agent to focus strictly on a **single, responsive local working page (`index.html`)** centered on the core product differentiator: **The Prescriptive AI Advisory Engine (PRD Section 7.2)**.
- **Key Features Implemented via Steering:**
  1. *Interactive Prescriptive Advisory Banner:* Displays real-time dynamic advisories (e.g., forecasting battery depletion before 4:15 AM based on load vs. sunlight).
  2. *One-Click Eco-Shedding Action:* Added an interactive `⚡ Apply Eco-Shedding` button that immediately sheds non-essential sanctuary A/C (3.10 kW), recalculates battery runtime in real time, and turns the advisory badge green.
  3. *Interactive Environmental Simulator:* Added live sliders for solar irradiance (sunlight intensity) and battery reserve level, allowing immediate simulation of overcast skies and low-battery alerts.
  4. *Facility Profile Switcher:* Added switching between Church Campus facilities (*Grace Cathedral*) and Member Enterprises (*Grace Bakery & Cold Store*), dynamically adapting circuits and load labels.
  5. *Mission Impact Tracker:* Integrated a tangible financial and diesel savings counter displaying litres of diesel saved and Naira (₦) redirected into ministry.
  6. *Client-Side Export:* Added an `Export Energy Audit (CSV)` feature using the browser Blob API, allowing instant data extraction without external servers.
